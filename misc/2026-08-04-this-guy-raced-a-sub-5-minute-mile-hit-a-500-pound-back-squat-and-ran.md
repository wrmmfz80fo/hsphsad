# This Guy Raced a Sub-5-Minute Mile, Hit a 500-Pound Back Squat, and Ran a Marathon in 1 Day

Last month, CrossFitter Adam Klink accomplished a legendary CrossFit feat by completing a 5-minute mile and 500-pound back squat in the same day. The challenge, first postulated by former CrossFit Games director Dave Castro in his book Constructing the CrossFit Games, was long held as an ultimate test of athleticism for the CrossFit community.

But Klink was only the first. Scottish hybrid athlete Fergus Crawley has also accomplished the challenge—and added an extra endurance test to go along with the benchmarks of speed and strength.

Unlike Klink, however, Crawley decided to do the lift first and the run second. Crawley gave the same reasoning as Klink, however: he wanted to accomplish the challenge he expected would be the toughest for him first.

In the video, Crawley starts his warmup light at around 9:30 a.m., building up to the final attempt. He worked up to 501 pounds for his epic squat, and nailed it.

"Yessss!" he yells after completing the rep. "Squat is done, and that was the part I was worried about, so feeling a lot better."

Next, Crawley heads to a track to complete his mile run at about 11:30 a.m. After some warmups, he and his pacer take off.

At 400 meters, he's at a 1:15 pace. But he speeds up, and as he crosses the finish line, he clocks in at 4:58, tripping over the finish line for good measure.

"Lesson learned: try to get over the line before you try to stop your watch," he says. "After all that, I go and trip on my spike at the finish."

After 10 minutes, he's still struggling to fully recover, and eventually pukes.

But Crawley set forth an additional challenge for his big day. Next, he's going to run a full marathon (26.2 miles), which he starts at 4 p.m.

"The plan is a sub-5 hour 50K," says Crawley. "

He straps on a running vest with lots of fuel and water, and heads out after "a lot of pizza, caffeine and some paracetamol".

At 5K in, he says "My legs feel like lead, I've got a mad stitch from the pizza I ate before this without enough time to digest, horrible headwind on the way out... but, we're moving."

At 10K, he gives a time update of 53:06. At 15K, he's at 1:22, and takes a rest. "I'm starting to feel the effects of the squat and the mile. My legs feel like lead, it's hot. This is going to hurt."

At 20K he is at 1:55, and at 24K, he's "cramping like mad in both of my quads."

He ditches his goal of a sub-5 hour marathon and the new goal is to "just finish". After downing some salt, he's back at it. But at 32K he is "fully cramping" and struggling to finish, only able to walk. He decides to get to 42.2K (which is 26.2 miles, the distance of a marathon) and call it a day. He completes that distance in a little over 5 hours.

"Big day, lots of energy systems tested and tried," he says.
