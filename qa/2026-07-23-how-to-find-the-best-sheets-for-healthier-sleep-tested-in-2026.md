# How to Find the Best Sheets for Healthier Sleep: Tested in 2026

Tired of Poor Sleep? Here Are Our Favorite Sheets for Night Sweats, Allergies, and Restless Sleep

Shopping for sheets sounds simple until you actually do it. Perhaps you’ve been dealing with sleepless nights, or waking up at 3 a.m. due to feeling too hot, too cold, or itchy. Your sheets’ material can have a huge impact on your sleep quality, and changing them up might be just the sleep hygiene adjustment you need to improve your shut-eye.

[1]

We looked at the sheet types you’re most likely to see when shopping. Then we evaluated them through the lens of real-life needs, like sleeping hot, sharing a bed, outfitting a deep mattress, or finding something gentle enough for sensitive skin or for people with asthma or allergies. We considered construction, feel, breathability, fit, ease of care, and overall value, along with firsthand testing for each pick. We also consulted experts on what shoppers should prioritize before making a purchase.

“Sheets can have an impact on your sleep because your environment matters, and what’s touching your skin can either support or disrupt it,” says Angela Holliday-Bell, MD, a board-certified pediatrician, sleep specialist, and founder of The Solution Is Sleep, based in the Greater Chicago Area.

Whether you want crisp, hotel-style bedding, a softer, lived-in feel, or sheets that keep you cool through the night, this guide should help you narrow down the options with confidence.

When to Consult a Doctor

Using the right sheets may help with comfort, temperature regulation, and irritation, but they can’t treat ongoing health issues. If you often have night sweats, skin flare-ups, congestion, wheezing, or disrupted sleep that doesn’t improve, check in with your healthcare professional. A doctor can help you figure out if bedding is part of the problem or if something else is going on.

Cooling Sheets

“As the body prepares for sleep, it naturally releases heat, especially from the hands, feet, and face. This cooling coincides with increased sleepiness. But when your environment is too warm, your body can’t shed heat effectively, making it harder to fall and stay asleep,” says Mike Gradisar, PhD, a clinical psychologist, research scientist, and head of sleep science at Sleep Cycle, based in Adelaide, Australia.

Cooling sheets are designed to help you sleep by reducing heat buildup and improving airflow around your body. Rather than referring to one single fabric, the term usually covers sheet sets made from materials known for breathability, moisture-wicking, or a cooler feel against skin. The right cooling material for you depends on the feel you prefer. Not every cooling sheet will feel cool to the touch, and some may prioritize softness over airflow or vice versa. If you tend to sleep hot, deal with night sweats, or just want bedding that feels lighter and less heat-trapping, this is one of the most useful categories to consider.

Deep-Pocket Sheets

Deep-pocket sheets are less about fabric feel and type and more about fit, making them a crucial category for shoppers with thicker mattresses. They also tend to be easier to put on, so possibly better for folks with mobility issues. If you use a pillow-top, a mattress topper, or prefer a bed that sits higher, standard fitted sheets may pop off the corners, or never quite feel secure to begin with. Deep-pocket sheets are designed to solve that problem with a deeper fitted-sheet depth. In general, deep-pocket sheets are made for mattresses that are thicker than standard, around 13 in or more. The best deep-pocket sheets still come down to the material and feel you prefer, but getting the right pocket depth is what keeps everything in place.

What Should I Look for if I Want Low-Maintenance Sheets?

In general, shoppers who want fuss-free bedding should look for sheets that can handle regular machine washing, don’t require special cycles beyond basic care, and are less likely to feel overly delicate after repeated use. Wrinkle resistance can also be a plus, though that tends to vary by material and weave.

Based on our testing, a few fabrics stood out as especially easy to live with. The Layla Bamboo Sheets and Origin Snow Silk Tencel Bedding Set were described as super-easy to care for, with no wrinkling and no special washing or drying required. The Brooklyn Bedding Deep Pocket Tencel Sheets also earned points for being easy to wash.

On the other hand, some sets are a bit higher-maintenance. The SIJO AiryWeight Eucalyptus Sheet Set needed a gentle cycle and had to be removed from the dryer quickly to avoid wrinkles, and the Bedsure PureWoven Organic Cotton Sheet Set can also wrinkle if left in the dryer too long. So if your top priority is convenience, it may be worth favoring cotton or smoother Tencel sets. You also might want to be a little more cautious with fabrics that wrinkle easily or need gentler laundering.
